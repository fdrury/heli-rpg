# Vision — working title: **ROTORWASH**

> One pilot. One airframe. A dead country, and something out there worth finding.

## The hook

Rotorcraft did not survive the collapse. Fixed-wing aircraft are rusting in place; a
helicopter is a machine that requires a living supply chain — fuel, bearings, seals,
blade balance, a person who understands it — and that chain broke. **You have the last
one that still flies.** Not because you are special: because you never stopped feeding it.

You are looking for someone specific. You have a name, a partial frequency, and a route
they were last flying. Everything else — the factions, the settlements, the contracts, the
wreck fields — is what you fly through to get there.

There are **no other helicopter pilots in the world.** This is a deliberate constraint, not
an oversight: it makes the player structurally unique, keeps flight encounters from turning
into dogfight spam, and makes every faction's interest in you legible without exposition.

## Pillars

1. **The aircraft is a character.** It is the second protagonist. It has a name, a history,
   accumulated damage, parts with provenance. Losing it is the real fail state.
2. **Flight is honest.** A blade-element rotor sim underneath, assists layered on top.
   Translational lift, torque, ground effect, vortex ring state and autorotation are
   *emergent*, not scripted. Assists can be peeled back to nothing.
3. **Knowledge is the progression.** No XP bars. You get better because you acquire
   *charts, frequencies, schematics, tools, contacts and parts* — see `02-progression.md`.
4. **Fog of war is the story.** The map starts near-empty. Flying reveals it; people tell
   you about it; old charts fill it in. The search drives the critical path and keeps
   scope honest.
5. **Everywhere in between.** Cities, wilderness, and — importantly — the ragged transitional
   country between them: exurbs, interchanges, industrial parks, drowned suburbs.

## Tone

Mature, melancholy, dryly funny. Violence has weight and consequence; it is never a joke
and never a spectacle. Adult themes handled seriously. No gore-for-shock, no grim-dark
nihilism. Closest reference points: *New Vegas*' writing voice, *Outer Wilds*' wistfulness,
*Nausicaä*'s relationship between a pilot and a broken world.

## Signature mechanic — "Rotor Time"

Tactical slow-motion, in the air and on the ground.

- **In flight:** at the apex of an aggressive manoeuvre — the top of a loop, a wingover, a
  pedal-turn — you can drop into slow-mo, zoom, and place shots on ground targets with the
  fixed forward guns. Costs a resource that refills through *committed* flying, so the game
  rewards the flamboyant manoeuvre that earns the shot.
- **On foot:** the same system as called shots against limbs, weapons and equipment.

It is one mechanic in two contexts, which keeps it cheap to build and makes the pilot feel
like the same person in and out of the cockpit.

## Non-goals

- Multiplayer. Ever.
- Commercial release. Free assets under copyleft/attribution licenses are fine and are
  tracked in `/ATTRIBUTIONS.md`.
- Charging the player (or the developer) per token for AI features. See `04-dialogue.md`.
